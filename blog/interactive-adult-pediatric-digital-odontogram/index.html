Dual Dentition Architecture • Adult & Pedo EMR

Interactive Adult (32-Tooth) vs. Pediatric (20-Tooth) Digital Odontograms: Clinical Design & Workflows

Direct Answer: Many general dental practices treat both adults and children, yet legacy dental software forces clinicians onto a static 32-tooth adult diagram. As detailed in our complete digital dental charting and odontogram guide, a true dual-dentition odontogram separates the 32-tooth permanent arch (`18–48`) from the 20-tooth deciduous arch (`55–85` / `A–T`) while preserving 5-surface clinical accuracy.

1. Why Pediatric Dentistry Requires a Dedicated 20-Tooth Primary Odontogram

Answer-First Summary: Primary dentition lacks premolars and third molars, consisting of 5 teeth per quadrant: central incisor, lateral incisor, canine, first primary molar, and second primary molar. Charting pediatric procedures—such as pulpotomies, glass ionomer cement (GIC) restorations, and preformed stainless steel crowns (SSCs)—on an adult chart corrupts insurance coding and clinical history.

In Clinsyst, selecting Pediatric (20 Teeth) during an examination dynamically renders Quadrants `5–8` (`55–51`, `61–65`, `85–81`, `71–75`) or Universal `A–T` (see our FDI vs. Universal Tooth Numbering Guide) with custom pediatric tooth anatomy.

2. 5-Surface Crown Mapping: M, D, O/I, B/La, and L/P

Answer-First Summary: Both adult and pediatric odontograms in Clinsyst allow multi-select surface tagging across all five anatomical crown zones: Mesial (`M`), Distal (`D`), Occlusal/Incisal (`O`), Buccal/Labial (`B`), and Lingual/Palatal (`L`), paired with severity grading (`Mild`, `Moderate`, `Severe`).

All surface findings feed directly into the patient's longitudinal medical record. Learn how these findings combine with allergy and periodontal alerts in Building a Unified Digital Patient Dossier: Medical History, Allergies & Alerts.
