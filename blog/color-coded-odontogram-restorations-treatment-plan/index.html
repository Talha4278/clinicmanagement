Visualizing Existing Restorations vs. Planned Treatments on a Color-Coded Odontogram

Direct Answer: Patients reject dental treatment plans when they cannot visualize what is wrong in their mouth. A color-coded digital dental charting odontogram turns abstract tooth numbers into an intuitive visual map—contrasting active pathology (`Red: Caries`, `Orange: Extraction`) against completed or restorative states (`Blue: Filling`, `Purple: RCT`, `Amber: Crown`, `Teal: Implant`).

1. The 8-Color Clinical Semantic Palette in Clinsyst

Answer-First Summary: Clinsyst assigns each clinical finding a distinct semantic color and SVG tooth visual state so clinicians and patients can scan all 32 adult or 20 pediatric teeth in under 3 seconds.
● Red (`#ef4444`)Active Caries
● Blue (`#3b82f6`)Composite / Filling
● Purple (`#8b5cf6`)Root Canal (RCT)
● Amber (`#f59e0b`)Crown / Bridge
● Orange (`#f97316`)Extraction Needed
● Teal (`#14b8a6`)Dental Implant
● Slate (`#94a3b8`)Missing Tooth
● Green (`#22c55e`)Sound / Healthy

2. Turning Chairside Visuals into Scheduled Procedures

Answer-First Summary: Showing the patient their color-coded arch on a chairside tablet increases multi-tooth treatment plan acceptance by making asymptomatic caries and uncrowded endodontically treated teeth visible and urgent.

Once the patient agrees to proceed, converting each red or purple tooth into a tracked procedure prevents revenue loss. See how to eliminate missed billing in Troubleshooting Missing Clinical Notes & Unlinked Procedures in Dental Practices.
