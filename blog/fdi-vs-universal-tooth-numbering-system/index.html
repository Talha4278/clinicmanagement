Clinical Charting Standards • ISO 3950 vs. ADA

FDI Two-Digit vs. Universal vs. Palmer Tooth Numbering Systems Explained (With Full Conversion Tables)

Direct Answer: Dental practices worldwide rely on three primary tooth notation systems: the FDI World Dental Federation Two-Digit System (ISO 3950), the ADA Universal Numbering System, and the Palmer Quadrant Notation. As part of our complete guide to digital dental charting and interactive odontograms, this reference maps every adult (`11–48` / `1–32`) and pediatric (`51–85` / `A–T`) tooth code across all three standards.

1. How the FDI Two-Digit System (ISO 3950) Works

Answer-First Summary: In the FDI system, every tooth is identified by a two-digit code (`[Quadrant][Tooth]`). The first digit indicates the quadrant (`1–4` for permanent teeth clockwise from upper right; `5–8` for primary pediatric teeth), and the second digit indicates the tooth position from the central midline (`1` central incisor to `8` third molar).

To prevent clinical confusion between spoken numbers like "sixteen" and "sixty", FDI numbers are always pronounced as two separate digits—for example, tooth `16` (Maxillary Right First Molar) is spoken as "one-six", and pediatric tooth `54` is spoken as "five-four".

2. Adult Permanent Dentition Conversion Table (32 Teeth: FDI vs. Universal)

Answer-First Summary: The ADA Universal system numbers adult teeth sequentially from `1` (Upper Right Third Molar, FDI `18`) across the maxillary arch to `16` (Upper Left Third Molar, FDI `28`), drops down to `17` (Lower Left Third Molar, FDI `38`), and finishes at `32` (Lower Right Third Molar, FDI `48`).

3. Pediatric Primary Dentition Conversion Table (20 Teeth: FDI 51–85 vs. Universal A–T)

Answer-First Summary: Primary (deciduous) teeth use Quadrants `5` (Upper Right), `6` (Upper Left), `7` (Lower Left), and `8` (Lower Right) in FDI (`51–85`), which map to uppercase letters `A` through `T` in Universal numbering (`55 = A`, `51 = E`, `61 = F`, `65 = J`, `75 = K`, `85 = T`).

When treating children in mixed dentition, toggling seamlessly between the 20-tooth pediatric chart and 32-tooth adult chart prevents charting a primary molar (`54` / `B`) on a permanent premolar slot (`14` / `5`).
